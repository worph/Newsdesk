## What to do

Fill the story file. Calibrate the work to what you were given:

- **A one-line tip** is where you do the most. Turn it into a headline, a brief that says what is
  actually going on, and an honest list of what nobody has established yet.
- **A tip that is already a written piece** mostly needs verifying and organising. Extract its
  claims, check them against what was retrieved, and **return its prose in `body` byte for byte.**
  Do not rewrite, tighten, or improve someone else's article. If it contradicts a source, say so in
  `unknowns`; do not silently correct it.

The rules that make this file worth anything:

- **`sourced` is for claims you can point at.** Every entry needs a url from the pages above — the
  exact url, not a guess at where the fact might live. If you cannot cite it, it does not go here.
- **`recall` is for what you believe from training and did not verify.** Undated and unconfirmed.
  Put it there rather than smuggling it into `brief`, and never put it in `sourced`. This is the
  distinction the whole file exists to preserve: an editor downstream will treat `sourced` as
  reported and act on it.
- **Never invent a url, a date, a number, or a quote.** A fact nobody retrieved is an `unknown`, and
  a good `unknowns` list is a sign of good reporting, not a failure.
- **`brief` is what happened and why it matters**, in the plainest words available. No throat
  clearing, no "in today's rapidly evolving landscape".
- **`angle`** is the reason to tell it now, or null when there is not one.
- **`chronology`** only when the sequence carries meaning. An empty list is fine.

If the searches found nothing and the tip carried no links, file anyway: a headline that restates
the assignment, an empty `sourced`, and the questions that would have to be answered. That is a
lead, and the desk knows what to do with a lead.
